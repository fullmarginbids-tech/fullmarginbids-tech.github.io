Markup vs. margin: why your 25% markup is only a 20% margin

If you add 25% to your costs thinking you're keeping a 25% margin, you're pricing short on every job. Here's the plain math, and how to fix it on your next bid.

Most owner-operated electrical, HVAC, and plumbing shops price the same way: add a percentage on top of cost. Nothing wrong with that, until you mix up two words that sound the same and aren't. Markup is the percentage you add to your cost. Margin is the percentage of the final price you actually keep. They are never equal, and the gap is real money.

The example that shows the gap

Say a job costs you $1,000 in labor and materials. You add a 25% markup:

  • Cost: $1,000
  • + 25% markup = $250
  • Price to customer: $1,250

Now look at what you kept. Your profit is $250, but it's $250 out of the $1,250 price, not out of the $1,000 cost. That's 250 / 1250 = 20%. You marked up 25% and walked away with a 20% margin. On a single job it's five points. Across a year of jobs, it's thousands you meant to keep and didn't.

The pattern: the margin is always lower than the markup, and the gap grows as your numbers grow.

How to price to a real target margin

Stop adding a markup and hoping. Decide the margin you want to keep, then back into the price with one formula:

price = cost / (1 - target margin)

Want to keep a 30% margin on that $1,000 job? 1000 / (1 - 0.30) = 1000 / 0.70 = $1,428.57. That's the price that actually leaves you 30%. A 30% markup would have priced it at $1,300 and left you only 23%.

Don't forget the costs that aren't on the quote

Margin math only works if "cost" is your true cost. The jobs that quietly lose money usually aren't underpriced labor. They're the line items that never made it onto the quote:

  • The permit and inspection time
  • Drive time, fuel, and loading the truck
  • Callbacks and warranty work
  • Material waste and small consumables
  • Your own pay and the overhead of running the business

Fold those into cost first, then apply your target margin. Miss two of them and a job that looked profitable is really break-even.
