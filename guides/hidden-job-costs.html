Won the job, lost the money? The costs that vanish from your quotes

The jobs that lose money usually aren't underpriced labor. They're the costs that never made it onto the quote. Here's how to catch them before you bid, not after.

Every owner-operator has had this one: you win the job, the work goes fine, and when it's all done the money just isn't there. It's confusing, because your labor rate looked right and your material markup looked right. The leak is almost never the obvious line. It's the handful of real costs that quietly fell off the estimate.

The five costs that disappear most

CostWhy it gets missed
Permits & inspection timeYou pay the fee and spend the hours, but only the fee makes the quote.
Drive time, fuel & loadingAn hour each way plus loading the truck is real paid time that rarely gets billed.
Callbacks & warrantyA small percentage of jobs come back. If none of your jobs carry a little for it, the ones that come back eat a whole margin.
Material waste & small consumablesWire nuts, staples, offcuts, the extra box. Individually tiny, collectively a line item.
Your own pay & overheadInsurance, the truck, the phone, the software, and YOU. If the job doesn't carry its share, the business does.

What missing two of them actually does

Say a job looks like this: $1,000 cost, priced at $1,250. Looks like a 20% margin ($250 profit). Now add back two costs you forgot: 2 hours of drive and load time at $45 = $90, and the permit time plus a small warranty allowance, say $110. Your real cost was $1,200, not $1,000.

So your true profit is 1250 - 1200 = $50, which is a 50 / 1250 = 4% margin, not 20%. The job didn't underprice labor. It carried two invisible costs, and they took three-quarters of the profit.

The fix: price off your TRUE cost

You can't mark up what you never counted. Before you apply any margin, build the cost with every real line in it: labor (including drive and load time), materials plus a waste factor, permit and inspection time, a small callback allowance, and your overhead share. Then price to the margin you actually want with one formula:

price = true cost / (1 - target margin)

Get the costing right and the margin takes care of itself. Get it wrong and no markup percentage will save the job.
